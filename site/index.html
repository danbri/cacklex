ShapesCore in the browser

One schema, one graph, two answers. Recursive shapes have a least-fixpoint reading (what SHACL is expected to adopt) and a greatest-fixpoint reading (what ShEx uses). This page runs the JavaScript mirror of the ShapesCore Lean core and shows both, plus whether they agree.

Verdicts

What this shows

LFP accepts a node only if there is a finite derivation: it is the semantics of reachability and of well-founded recursion. GFP accepts unless there is a finite refutation: it is the semantics of invariants and of ShEx. Where the two agree, every supported-model reading agrees too (Lean theorem sms_determined). GFP is computed here as the complement of LFP of the dual catalogue (gfp_iff_not_lfp_dual). Atoms available: isA, isB, isC, isD, which test the node's name.

Only the common core of SHACL and ShEx is implemented: node tests, and, or, shape names, and counting over one predicate step. Nothing SHACL-only or ShEx-only is here. The Lean side, with 107 theorems, is at danbri/cacklex.

Everything runs in your browser. The mirror is unverified; the theorem names it cites are checked by Lean.
